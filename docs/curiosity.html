A curiosity: the ratio √10 / 3

In the construction, a right triangle with legs 1 and 1/3 keeps appearing; it carries Holden's angle arctan(1/3). Its hypotenuse is √10/3 ≈ 1.05409. As a frequency ratio this is very close to a semitone. This page lets you measure and hear how close, without claiming more than the numbers show.

Two facts stand out. The square of √10/3 is exactly 10:9, the just “minor whole tone” of Ptolemy's syntonic diatonic, so √10/3 is exactly half of that tone. And it lies within about 1 cent of the Pythagorean semitone, the limma 256:243, whereas the equal-tempered semitone is almost 9 cents away.

Half a schisma: the exact link to the limma

The “about 1 cent” between √10/3 and the limma has an exact name. Square both sides:

(√10/3)² = 10/9 = (256/243)² × 32805/32768

(check: 65536 × 32805 / (59049 × 32768) = 65610/59049 = 10/9). The last factor, 32805/32768 = 1.954 cents, is the schisma of tuning theory. So √10/3 is the limma widened by exactly half a schisma, 0.977 cents: the minor whole tone 10:9 is two limmas and one schisma.

Twelve pure fifths 3:2 overshoot seven octaves by the Pythagorean comma, which is why a circle of pure fifths never closes. Twenty-four of these half-schismas (twelve schismas) come within 0.015 cents of that comma, so narrowing each pure fifth by one schisma, that is by (√10/3 ÷ limma)², gives a fifth of 700.0013 cents: equal temperament's fifth to a thousandth of a cent, and a circle that misses closing by only 0.015 cents. You can hear it on The chord wheel (Pentametric circle of fifths, fifth: Kirnberger).

exact the identity is plain arithmetic. known the schisma, the near-equality of twelve schismas and the comma, and the fifth 16384/10935 are classical tuning theory (J. P. Kirnberger, 18th century, proposed this fifth as a whole-number stand-in for equal temperament). What is new here is only the route: from the triangle with legs 1 and 1/3 to that fifth. No fraction of whole numbers can close the circle exactly, since 27/12 is irrational; and √10 only brings square roots, never a 12th root of 2.

Can it replace the equal-tempered semitone?

An equal temperament must close at the octave: twelve equal steps have to make exactly 2:1. Twelve steps of 21/12 give 1200 cents. Twelve steps of √10/3 give only 1094.4 cents, about a semitone short of the octave; it would take 13.16 of them to reach 2:1. So √10/3 cannot be the step of a scale that repeats at the octave. It could be the step of a non-octave scale, a family that composers have explored (for example Wendy Carlos's alpha and beta scales, or the Bohlen–Pierce scale), and that is a legitimate artistic direction rather than a correction of equal temperament.

A curiosity: the ratio √10 / 3

In the construction, a right triangle with legs 1 and 1/3 keeps appearing; it carries Holden's angle arctan(1/3). Its hypotenuse is √10/3 ≈ 1.05409. As a frequency ratio this is very close to a semitone. This page lets you measure and hear how close, without claiming more than the numbers show.

The numbers

hypotenuse
√(1 + 1/9) = √10/3 = 1.0540925534
its square
10/9 exactly
size in cents
600 · log₂(10/9) = 91.20
angle at E
arctan(1/3) ≈ 18.43°

A cent is 1/100 of an equal-tempered semitone; most listeners notice differences of about 5 cents or more between two steady tones.

Compared with the semitones of music

intervalratiocentsdifference from √10/3listen

Two facts stand out. The square of √10/3 is exactly 10:9, the just “minor whole tone” of Ptolemy's syntonic diatonic, so √10/3 is exactly half of that tone. And it lies within about 1 cent of the Pythagorean semitone, the limma 256:243, whereas the equal-tempered semitone is almost 9 cents away.

Can it replace the equal-tempered semitone?

An equal temperament must close at the octave: twelve equal steps have to make exactly 2:1. Twelve steps of 21/12 give 1200 cents. Twelve steps of √10/3 give only 1094.4 cents, about a semitone short of the octave; it would take 13.16 of them to reach 2:1. So √10/3 cannot be the step of a scale that repeats at the octave. It could be the step of a non-octave scale, a family that composers have explored (for example Wendy Carlos's alpha and beta scales, or the Bohlen–Pierce scale), and that is a legitimate artistic direction rather than a correction of equal temperament.

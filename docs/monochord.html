The monochord and the tetractys

The Pythagoreans tested consonance on a single string with a movable bridge. Stopping the string at half its length sounds the octave, at two thirds the fifth, at three quarters the fourth. These ratios, 2:1, 3:2 and 4:3, are the ratios of neighbouring rows of the tetractys, the triangle of 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10 points. The Pentametric Cycle produces exactly these ratios: the residue H picks rows H and H − 1.

The sounding length

A string half as long vibrates twice as fast: the pitch ratio is the inverse of the length ratio. The tetractys has four rows, so neighbouring rows give exactly three ratios, 2:1, 3:2 and 4:3; with the unison 1:1 these are the four consonances of the cycle.

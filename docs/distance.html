Distance to the spiral

How far is the Fibonacci spiral from the Origin, the point P where Holden's two lines meet? Move along the spiral (drag on the picture, use the slider, or press play) and watch the distance, measured in Holden units (h = √0.1, the side of Holden's grid). It is a whole number of units exactly where the spiral crosses Holden's lines: 2, 4, 6, 10, 16, 26, … twice the Fibonacci numbers. You can also follow the Pentametric spiral, through the centres of all the squares, and see where its squares' corners fall. This page is an exploration beyond the papers.

The distance along the spiral

Gold: the crossings with Holden's lines (whole numbers of units). Blue: the joins, where one quarter-circle meets the next. With the Pentametric spiral chosen, its curve (purple) is drawn over the Fibonacci one (pale): it meets Holden's lines at the Lucas numbers. On the logarithmic scale the curve becomes almost a straight staircase: each quarter turn multiplies the distance by about φ = 1.618…, the golden ratio, as for a true golden spiral.

The squares' corners: the ±0.8 as a distance

In the pentagon picture of the Pentametric spiral, square n sits n Holden units from the Origin with its diagonal along its ray. A square of area n²/5 would touch the Origin exactly with its inner corner. The real square has area 𝔓(n) = (n² + 4χ)/5, where χ = (n | 5) is 0, +1 or −1, so its half-diagonal is √(n² + 4χ) units, and its inner corner misses the Origin by n − √(n² + 4χ) Holden units: past it when χ = +1, short of it when χ = −1, exactly on it when 5 divides n. This is the ±0.8 of the formula, seen as a distance.

The miss for every n from 1 to 60 (above the line: short of the Origin; below: past it), with ±2/n dashed. Gold rings: the Lucas numbers.

proved At the Lucas numbers the miss is exactly 2ψk = ±2/φk Holden units (ψ = (1 − √5)/2): −1.236, +0.764, −0.472, +0.292, −0.180, +0.111, … It changes side every time and shrinks by the golden ratio. Proof: χ(Lk) = −(−1)k and Lk2 − 4(−1)k = 5Fk2, so the miss is Lk − √5·Fk = (φk + ψk) − (φk − ψk) = 2ψk. For the other n it is close to ∓2/n.

What the distance shows

  • Crossings: twice the next Fibonacci number. In the square of side Fk the spiral crosses Holden's line once, at exactly 2Fk+1 Holden units from the Origin: 2, 4, 6, 10, 16, 26, 42, … Each crossing sits at the same place in its quarter turn, 53.13° in, the angle arctan(4/3) of the 3-4-5 triangle: seen from the centre of its arc, every crossing is the same 3-4-5 point, scaled by Fk (the Epitetartic Rhombus; note doi:10.5281/zenodo.23325442). proved
  • Joins: Pythagoras with two crossings. Where the arc of square j meets the arc of square j + 1, the distance is 2√F2j+1 units: √8, √20, √52, √136, √356, … Since F2j+1 = Fj2 + Fj+12, this is √((2Fj)2 + (2Fj+1)2): the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are the distances of the crossings in squares j − 1 and j. proved (the join is the arc's end, Mj − Fjij; its squared distance is (2Fj)² + (Fj + Lj)² units², and Fj + Lj = 2Fj+1.) Checked for j ≤ 27.
  • The closest point. The spiral starts 2 units from the Origin, comes in to its closest point, √10 − √2 = 1.748 units, at 29.5% of the first quarter turn (where the line from the arc's centre through the Origin meets the arc), and from there on it only moves away. proved for the minimum; computed that the distance only grows after it.
  • The Pentametric spiral. Through the centres of all the squares n, it is exactly n Holden units from the Origin everywhere, by construction, and it meets Holden's lines at the Lucas numbers (the Fibonacci squares' centres, Lk units: Holden). In the same square the Fibonacci spiral crosses the line Fk units further out: 2Fk+1 = Lk + Fk. proved
  • The corners. The inner corner of square n misses the Origin by n − √(n² + 4χ) units, exactly 2ψk = ±2/φk at the Lucas numbers. proved
  • Growth by φ. Distances at the same place of successive quarter turns grow in the ratio Fk+2/Fk+1 → φ: the Fibonacci spiral, made of circle arcs, approaches the golden logarithmic spiral around the Origin.

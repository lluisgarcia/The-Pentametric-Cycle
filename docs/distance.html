Distance to the spiral

How far is the Fibonacci spiral from the Origin, the point P where Holden's two lines meet? Move along the spiral (drag on the picture, use the slider, or press play) and watch the distance, measured in Holden units (h = √0.1, the side of Holden's grid). It is a whole number of units exactly where the spiral crosses Holden's lines: 2, 4, 6, 10, 16, 26, … twice the Fibonacci numbers. This page is an exploration beyond the papers.

The distance along the spiral

Gold: the crossings with Holden's lines (whole numbers of units). Blue: the joins, where one quarter-circle meets the next. On the logarithmic scale the curve becomes almost a straight staircase: each quarter turn multiplies the distance by about φ = 1.618…, the golden ratio, as for a true golden spiral.

What the distance shows

  • Crossings: twice the next Fibonacci number. In the square of side Fk the spiral crosses Holden's line once, at exactly 2Fk+1 Holden units from the Origin: 2, 4, 6, 10, 16, 26, 42, … Each crossing sits at the same place in its quarter turn, 53.13° in, the angle arctan(4/3) of the 3-4-5 triangle: seen from the centre of its arc, every crossing is the same 3-4-5 point, scaled by Fk (the Epitetartic Rhombus; note doi:10.5281/zenodo.23325443). proved
  • Joins: Pythagoras with two crossings. Where the arc of square j meets the arc of square j + 1, the distance is 2√F2j+1 units: √8, √20, √52, √136, √356, … Since F2j+1 = Fj2 + Fj+12, this is √((2Fj)2 + (2Fj+1)2): the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are the distances of the crossings in squares j − 1 and j. proved (the join is the arc's end, Mj − Fjij; its squared distance is (2Fj)² + (Fj + Lj)² units², and Fj + Lj = 2Fj+1.) Checked for j ≤ 27.
  • The closest point. The spiral starts 2 units from the Origin, comes in to its closest point, √10 − √2 = 1.748 units, at 29.5% of the first quarter turn (where the line from the arc's centre through the Origin meets the arc), and from there on it only moves away. proved for the minimum; computed that the distance only grows after it.
  • Growth by φ. Distances at the same place of successive quarter turns grow in the ratio Fk+2/Fk+1 → φ: the Fibonacci spiral, made of circle arcs, approaches the golden logarithmic spiral around the Origin.

Coordinates as in the note: Gardiner's frame, first square centred at (0, 0), Origin P = (1/10, 3/10), centre of square k at P + ik−1Lkw with w = −(1 + 3i)/10, and its arc centred at the corner Mk = Ck + Fkik−1(1 + i)/2 with radius Fk. Idea and question: Lluis Garcia Torcal.

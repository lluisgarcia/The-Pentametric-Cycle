Ὁ Πενταμετρικὸς Κύκλος

The Pentametric Cycle

𝔓(n)♪(n)
𝔓(n) = n² + 4 C(m)5, m = 4n² mod 10, C(m) = 26m − 5m²24
𝔓(Lk) = Fk²

The formula as found. C(m) equals the Legendre symbol n5 (0 or ±1, not a fraction), so 𝔓(n) = (n² + 4·n5) / 5. ♪(n) is the interval of 𝔓(n) mod 5.

A formula found by experiment on the squares of the Fibonacci spiral. At every Lucas number 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, 18, … it returns the square of the matching Fibonacci number, and nowhere else is it a perfect square. Reduced modulo 5, it becomes a palindrome of the four Pythagorean consonances: unison, octave, fifth and fourth.

 Animation · 2D and 3D

The Pentametric spiral

Squares of area 𝔓(n) turning 72° per step around a pentagon. The Lucas squares swing into the golden rectangle, the Fibonacci spiral grows inside it with sound, and in 3D everything grows in time.

 Note · 5 : 4

The Epitetartic Rhombus

A rhombus with diagonals 5 : 4, found where Holden's line meets an arc of the spiral, and held by every square: its construction with a ruler, and why halving never finds its corner while threes and fives do.

 Note · distance

Distance to the spiral

Move along the Fibonacci spiral and watch its distance from the Origin in origins units: whole numbers exactly on Holden's lines (2, 4, 6, 10, 16, …), Pythagoras at the joins, growth by φ.

 Note · origins

The origin of origins

Of all the circles about the centre of the unit square, only one has a tangent through a corner where it crosses a curved diagonal: area π/10. Its eight crossings are the origins of eight Fibonacci spirals, and Holden's lines are its tangent and radius there.

 Note · cells

Counting the areas

From an empty square and a ruler: the square of area 1/5, the circle of origins, Holden's lines and a grid of cells of 1/10 nobody drew. Then 𝔓(n) is a count of cells: the blue square of n, plus or minus the blue square of 2.

 Note · landing

Where the origin lands

Step Holden's crossing out, H, 2H, 3H, … and it always lands exactly on an origin of a unit square, turned 0, ¾, ¼, ½ of a turn, or on a corner every fifth step: the breath as a place, and a quarter turn per Fibonacci square.

 Note · hidden origin

The hidden origin

Run the Fibonacci spiral backwards with squares of half the size: it leads in smoothly from H′ = (7/10, −1/10), the mirror of Holden's crossing and an origin of the next square. Other ratios, the lines of slopes 7 and −1/7, and the golden pole.

When silence becomes 5

The consonance indicator H = 𝔓 mod 5 takes the values 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 0 is the silence. Lluis reads the groups of five differently: their members are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 5 is the fifth member, not 0 (0 means nothing, or a full cycle). Written as a formula, 1 + MOD(𝔓 − 1; 5): every silence becomes a 5. This page follows what that changes. It is an exploration beyond the papers: the reading is an interpretation; the results below are labelled.

1 · Two ways to count to five

One period, n = 0 … 24. The usual remainder H gives 0 at the 9 silences; Lluis's grouping gives 5 there (purple) and leaves every other value unchanged. Nothing is lost: 5 and 0 are the same class mod 5. What changes is that 5 can be used: as a ratio, a harmonic, a note.

2 · Reading A: the silences become major thirds

The paper reads H as the ratio H : (H − 1), the consecutive ratios of the tetractys: 1:1, 2:1, 3:2, 4:3, with 0 as silence. Read the silence as 5 and the same rule gives 5 : 4, the just major third, the next superparticular ratio after the tetractys. It completes the major triad (1, 5/4, 3/2) and is the arithmetic mean of 1:1 and 3:2. Each period then sounds 9 major thirds and 4 of each of the other intervals.

n = 0 … 50. Each column sounds two notes in just intonation: the fundamental and the interval above it. Purple: the major thirds that were silences.

3 · Reading B: the steps, and a natural trumpet

Lluis's second reading divides each value by the one before it: i(n)/i(n − 1). With the usual remainder this breaks at every silence (division by 0, or a ratio of 0); with the silence as 5 every step is a ratio. And because the steps multiply in a chain, the note at step n is just i(n) times a fundamental: the cycle plays only the harmonics 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 of one note (in C: C, C, G, C, E going up), like a natural trumpet or a horn call, and each step is a leap between two of them.

proved The 25 steps of a period multiply to exactly 1: the melody comes home every 25 steps. (They are i(1)/i(0) · i(2)/i(1) · … · i(25)/i(24) = i(25)/i(0) = 5/5.) Click a row or a point to hear that step.

4 · The leaps of the palindrome

The notes of the palindrome read the same backwards, so the horn call is its own retrograde. In its leaps this shows as a mirror with a twist: because i(50 − n) = i(n), the leap at step 51 − n is the reciprocal of the leap at step n, step(51 − n) = i(n − 1)/i(n). The second half makes the leaps of the first half in reverse order and the other way up: every rise of the first half is a fall of the same size in the second. proved

And the mirror repeats, like a fractal (Lluis's observation). Inside the first half the leaps mirror the same way about step 13, the single unison leap where the fifth sounds twice (3, 3 at n = 12 | 13): step(26 − k) = 1/step(k). Step 12 goes down, step 14 goes up; step 10 goes up, step 16 goes down; and so on. So the first 12 leaps, taken together, go down (×3/5, from harmonic 5 to harmonic 3) and the next 12 go up by the same amount (×5/3). The second half does the same about step 38. Then the whole block of 24 around step 13 is mirrored again by the block around step 38, across the big axis: step 12 (down) meets step 39 (up), and the inner part 14–37 mirrors itself. proved

The leaps, up (green) or down (red), height in octaves (log₂ of the step). Each shaded column or dashed line is a mirror: across it every bar meets a bar of the same size and opposite sign. Yellow arcs cross the small mirrors (steps 13, 38, 63, 88), blue arcs the mirrors between halves (25|26, 75|76), red arcs the mirror between blocks of 50 (50|51).

Why, and how far it goes. The notes have two mirrors: i(−n) = i(n), because 𝔓(−n) = 𝔓(n), and i(25 − n) = i(n), because 𝔓(25 − n) − 𝔓(n) = 125 − 10n, a multiple of 5. Two mirrors 12½ apart make a repeat of 25 and an endless row of mirrors, one every 12½ notes: on the silences (n = 0, 25, 50, …) and between the repeated fifths (12½, 37½, …). For the leaps that puts a mirror at 13, 25½, 38, 50½, 63, … for ever. So the block of 25 mirrors the next 25, the block of 50 the next 50, the block of 100 the next 100, ad infinitum, as Lluis says (checked by computer to 1000 notes). proved Strictly, a fractal also repeats at smaller and smaller sizes. Here the mirrors repeat at every larger size but stop at 12½: the first 12 leaps are not a mirror of themselves. So it is self-similar upwards, like a frieze of mirrors. interpretation

5 · Which intervals, how often

Brought into one octave, the 25 steps of a period are all consonances of just intonation, and no dissonance. That is not special to the cycle: any ratio of two numbers from 1 to 5 is consonant, which is the old principle of Zarlino's senario (1558, the numbers 1 to 6). What the cycle chooses is which consonances, how often and in what order: thirds and sixths most, the fourth and the fifth once each.

The 9 unisons and octaves equal the 9 silences of a period: a coincidence of counting, not a cause (they come from different steps).

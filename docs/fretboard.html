The mirror fretboard

A guitar's fretboard is several monochords side by side, and the frets measure the string logarithmically: every fret shortens the sounding length by the same ratio, so equal steps of pitch are unequal steps of length. In all-fourths tuning (E A D G C F) every string is a fourth above the one before, and the pattern of intervals repeats the same way all over the board. Lluis noticed something more: the board is a mirror. Reflect it through any root and every interval lands on its inversion, as in the palindrome.

2 · Why it is a mirror

On string s (counting from the lowest, s = 0) at fret f, an all-fourths board sounds the note low + 5s + f semitones. Reflecting a place through a centre (s₀, f₀) sends (s, f) to (2s₀ − s, 2f₀ − f), and the two notes add up to twice the centre: one lies exactly as far below the centre as the other lies above it. When the centre is a root, an interval of x semitones above the root is reflected to x below it, which is 12 − x above: its inversion. So the mirror partners are R ↔ R, ♭2 ↔ 7, 2 ↔ ♭7, ♭3 ↔ 6, 3 ↔ ♭6, 4 ↔ 5, and the tritone ♭5 is its own partner. proved

Every root of the board is such a centre, and the pattern also repeats every 12 frets (the octave) and one string up, five frets back (the same note). proved All-fifths tuning (C G D A E B) is the same board with the strings a fifth apart; it is still a mirror. Standard tuning is not: its B string is a major third above G, so tick check the whole mirror with standard tuning and the pairs across that string turn red. proved

The link to the palindrome. A pattern that goes round a circle of N places and reads the same backwards from 0 always has a second mirror half way round. On the 12 notes the two mirrors are the root and the tritone (0 and 6, which stay where they are); in the Pentametric palindrome they are n = 0 and n = 25 in every 50 (and the inner ones 12½ apart). In both, an interval and its inversion are partners across the mirror: on this board 4 ↔ 5, the fourth and the fifth, which are exactly the two leaps that the palindrome turns into each other (see The palindrome on Euler's lattice). interpretation

3 · The palindrome on the board

Each step of the cycle sounds the interval H : (H − 1) above the root: the unison and the octave are the root (R), the fifth 3:2 is 5, the fourth 4:3 is 4, and with the silence read as 5 the major third 5:4 is 3. The board lights every place of that interval. The fifth and the fourth, the two consonances the cycle balances (four of each in every period), are mirror partners on the board. All-fourths tuning and its regular pattern are known to guitarists, and regular tunings are a case of what theorists call isomorphic layouts: the same shape plays the same interval everywhere. The mirror reading, and its link to the palindrome, are Lluis's (from his 2024 study of the matrix of fifths, the all-fourths fretboard and Coltrane's circle). This page is a tool that will grow.

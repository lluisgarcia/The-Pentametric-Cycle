How to read this

In this lab, Radius is a custom geometric label: it is whichever trig value you choose, and that value is the length of one side of the right triangle. Standard definitions never change: SIN = Opp/Hyp, COS = Adj/Hyp, TAN = Opp/Adj, SEC = 1/COS, CSC = 1/SIN, COT = 1/TAN.

There are only three ways to normalize a triangle: Hypotenuse = 1 (Radius = SIN → Opposite, or COS → Adjacent), Adjacent = 1 (Radius = SEC → Hypotenuse, or TAN → Opposite) and Opposite = 1 (Radius = CSC → Hypotenuse, or COT → Adjacent). The scale factor k is 1, 1/COS(θ) or 1/SIN(θ), and every side is simply k × (Adj, Opp, Hyp) of the unit-circle triangle — which keeps everything consistent in all four quadrants.

With Hypotenuse = 1 and Radius = SIN(θ), the Radius and the Opposite side coincide because Radius was defined that way — it is not the conventional unit-circle radius, which is the hypotenuse.
